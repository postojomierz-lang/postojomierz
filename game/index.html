PLASTIC FRONT

Tip out the toy box. Spend your supply on soldiers, vehicles, aircraft and fortifications, then watch the plastic armies fight it out. Each round brings reinforcements. The last headquarters standing wins.

Advanced: let Claude design a toy-room battlefield (needs your own Anthropic API key)

Your description is sent to Anthropic's Claude API and billed to your key. The key is saved only in this browser; use one with a spending limit. If anything fails you get a random battlefield instead.

PLAY ONLINE

Battle your friends over the internet. One player hosts and shares the room code or invite link, the others join. Armies nobody plays are commanded by the computer. The host's setup screen decides the map, the number of armies and the difficulty.

Connection settings

Browsers try to connect directly first. If a network blocks that (some office, school or mobile networks), the game messages go through a TURN relay. By default that is the free Open Relay by Metered — no account needed. You can use your own relay instead.

Everyone in a game should use the same relay settings. Your settings stay in this browser.

Controls

CameraLeft-drag or WASD to pan · right-drag or Q/E to rotate · wheel or pinch to zoom BuildPick a piece at the bottom, click or drag inside your zone · R rotates walls · right-click removes a piece placed this round · Ctrl+Z undo BattleSpace starts the battle / pauses · 1, 2, 4 change speed RoundsEach battle lasts 40 seconds. Survivors stay where they are; you then get more supply to reinforce. Up to 3 vehicles and 2 aircraft per round. AircraftFighters hunt aircraft, attack planes hit tanks and guns, bombers make one bombing run near the nearest enemy HQ, transports drop 6 paratroopers. Counter them with anti-air guns, AA missile soldiers and your own fighters. OrdersEach round pick a main target (the drop-down, or click an army in the list on the right): your vehicles, bombers and most of your infantry march on that army (a few riflemen closest to the HQ stay home to guard it), but still shoot anyone who comes in range, and turn back if your base is invaded. Defend keeps everyone inside your zone to guard the HQ. The list also shows who every army is going for. CreditsAll figures, vehicles, aircraft, buildings, emplacements, fortifications and scenery were modelled for the game in Blender. Online“Play online” on the setup screen: host a room and send the code or invite link, or join a friend's room. Everyone deploys at the same time and presses Ready; the battle starts when all players are ready. If someone leaves, the computer takes over their army. If a network blocks direct connections, the game goes through a free relay (TURN) automatically — see Connection settings in the online dialog. WoundedSoldiers often go down wounded instead of dying (red cross). A nearby comrade drags them behind the closest cover (a book, a mug, sandbags), medics patch them up on the spot so they fight on, and field ambulances pick up to 4 from safe spots and drive them back to base — they steer clear of enemy fire. Anyone saved fights again next round for free. Wounded left out in the open bleed out. NationsPick your army's nation on the setup screen (and your opponents'). Every nation fights with the same rules and numbers; what changes is how its soldiers, vehicles, aircraft and headquarters look — Americans with M1 helmets, Garands, Shermans and Mustangs; Germans with Stahlhelms, MG 42s, Panzerschrecks, Panzer IVs, Tigers, Stukas and a concrete command bunker; the Red Army with SSh-40 helmets, Mosins, DP-28s, PTRD anti-tank rifles, T-34s, IS-2s, Katyushas, Il-2s and a log dugout command post; the British with Brodie helmets, Lee-Enfields, Brens, PIATs, Bren carriers, Cromwells, Churchills, Spitfires, Typhoons, Lancasters and a Nissen hut; the Japanese with Type 90 helmets and neck flaps, puttees, Arisakas with long bayonets, knee mortars, Ha-Gos, Chi-Has, Zeros, Vals, Bettys and a field headquarters house; the French with Adrian helmets, puttees, MAS-36 rifles with spike bayonets, FM 24/29s, kepis for officers, R35s, Somuas, Char B1s, D.520s, Breguets, LeO 451s and a stone farmhouse; the Italians with M33 helmets, side caps for officers, Carcanos, Breda 30s, 'red devil' grenades, Solothurn anti-tank rifles, AB 41s, M13/40s, P40s, Folgores, SM.79s and a farmhouse with its dovecote tower. Each army is moulded in its nation's colour: American green, German field grey, Soviet red, British khaki tan, Japanese white, French horizon blue and Italian black (a second army of the same nation takes a free colour). Its vehicles fly its flag from a staff at the back (the Germans the Balkenkreuz on field grey). TanksThree classes. The light tank (M5 Stuart) is fast and cheap, with a quick-firing small gun for infantry and cars, but thin armour. The medium tank (M4 Sherman) is the all-rounder. The heavy tank (M26 Pershing) is slow and expensive, very hard to knock out, and its long gun outranges the others. Bazookas and anti-tank mines are the answer to all of them. EngineersEngineer trucks (Vehicles) carry two sappers. In battle they drive out along the way the enemy is most likely to come and set up a belt across it a few cells in front of your zone (right in front of it when you Defend): 4 steel tank traps (vehicles cannot pass until they shoot them away; infantry walks through), 5 anti-tank mines (blow up the first enemy vehicle on them) or 6 anti-personnel mines (go off under enemy soldiers). Mines are hidden from the enemy and stay in the ground from round to round; the truck drives home and is restocked every round. Friendly fireExplosions hurt everyone nearby, including your own soldiers. Troops avoid lobbing grenades or shells at enemies standing next to friends, but misses happen. ScenariosPick a historical battle from the list (grouped by front) to fight it on a fitting battlefield, with a short briefing. The battle itself stays fair: every army gets the same ground. Or choose a free battle and pick the battlefield yourself. MapsThe Normandy countryside is a new diorama every game: fields boxed in by hedgerows (they block movement and sight; look for the gates), lanes, farms, stone walls and hay bales for cover. The Town in ruins has streets, houses, a square and often a river with bridges: infantry can get into ruined houses and fight from them with good cover; vehicles have to use the streets. On the Beach landing every army comes ashore on its own beach and fights towards the bunkers round the lighthouse; trenches, pillboxes and Tobruk pits shelter infantry the same way. In the Winter forest infantry can move and hide among the trees (you cannot see far into a forest); vehicles keep to the lanes and clearings. In the Desert, rocky outcrops and burnt-out tanks block the way, escarpments and dunes give low cover and stone sangars shelter infantry. The Jungle works like the forest, with a river crossed by wooden bridges and log bunkers to fight from. In the Mountains the massifs block the way; fight through the passes to the fortress, whose walls you can shoot over. Or pick the classic toy room: a random floor, the map library, or a map file a friend sent you. "Save map" (top bar) saves a toy-room battlefield; the Map editor lets you build your own. MinimapClick the minimap (bottom right) to jump the camera there. WinningDestroy the enemy headquarters. After 10 rounds the strongest army wins.
